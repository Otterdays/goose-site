Progress log

What changed, newest first, grouped by day.

  • 2026-10-29

    An editor tutorial.

    A new Tutorial button in the editor's top bar opens a checklist of eleven steps, from flying around to looking at a model. Each step ticks itself when you do it, and you can also tick it by hand; a bar shows how many are done. Ticks are not saved, so they clear when the editor closes. Checked by tests; not yet looked at in the running window.

    The C615 preview and pose detection work again.

    A workbench screenshot shows device 0 at 1280 × 720 over DirectShow, with a pose found at about 10 frames per second. Leg visibility is still very low and no take samples have been recorded in this check, so the camera needs to be positioned farther back or aimed to include the full body.

    Build how things look, with textures.

    A new Models tab lets you make a tree, a workbench or a rock out of boxes, cylinders and ramps that snap together, then paint them with colours and patterns such as planks, bricks and speckle. Point at a part and it lights up, click to select it, right-click for a menu, and drag arrows, squares or a ring to move, stretch or turn it. Each look is saved as a small text file, and the map and the game draw things from those files. Checked by tests and a start-up run; not yet looked at in the running window.

    The webcam workbench now starts with the known C615 camera index.

    It defaults to index 0, used for an earlier successful capture, and the guide says to close Logitech Camera Settings before previewing. A screenshot shows that app has a live image while the workbench reports unavailable, so camera contention is a possibility to check.

    The webcam workbench now flags a blank camera feed.

    A camera can open but still send a black picture. The tool now stops and explains that problem instead of reporting that no person was found, so a bad camera source is easier to spot.

    A workbench for webcam pose capture.

    Open launch-mocap.bat to check the camera, watch the pose overlay, save joint estimates and review how well the body stayed tracked. It never saves camera video. Smoothing, retargeting and animation export are still future steps.

    A first webcam movement sample is captured.

    The local camera tool saved joint positions for about two and a half minutes, without saving camera video. It found a person in most frames, but often missed the legs, so the room and camera framing need another pass before this is ready for animation.

    The Goose cape no longer vanishes at an angle.

    The engine hides the back of flat surfaces, so the cape needed visible cloth faces on both sides. I fixed the mesh and changed its preview to hide the same faces as the engine. The updated checks and six-angle render pass; not yet seen in the running window.

    The Goose cape stays visible from the front.

    Two white chest panels now join the shoulder wrap, so the torso no longer hides the whole cape in a front view. The updated item checks and six-angle render pass; not yet seen in the running window.

    A Goose cape that wraps from back to front.

    The creator now has a separate Goose cape toggle. Its white feather mantle, black shoulder wrap and orange bill clasp were refined through eight visual rounds until the front and back connected cleanly. The item render checks pass; not yet seen in the running window.

    Place tall grass.

    A new Grass tufts tool plants clumps of knee-high grass blades. Click to plant one, drag to plant a trail, and tick Erase to pull them up. They are saved in the map. They are for touches of tall grass here and there (up to 5,000 per map); you can walk straight through them for now. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    Spray-paint grass on the ground.

    The editor has a new Paint ground tool. Hold the mouse and drag to spray grass onto the ground. A round ring follows the mouse to show where it will land, and a slider (or the [ and ] keys) makes it bigger or smaller. An Erase box scrapes grass off again. The grass is saved in the map, so the game shows it too. Grass is the only texture so far. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    Crouch.

    Hold Ctrl to crouch: the knees bend, the body drops, and you move slowly. You cannot run or jump while crouched. Checked by tests; not yet looked at in the running game.

    Run and jump.

    Hold Shift to run at twice walking speed and press Space to jump about knee height. There are new Run and Jump animations, and the character switches between them by itself. Checked by tests; not yet looked at in the running game, so the poses may need touching up.

    A main menu and a pause menu.

    The game now opens on a menu with Play and Quit. Esc no longer quits: it opens a pause menu with Resume, Main menu and Quit. The world keeps running behind the menu, because this is meant to become an online world that does not stop when one player pauses. Checked by tests; not yet looked at in the running window.

    The game has rules.

    You can now chop trees (hold E), watch them fall to a stump and grow back, collect wood, make planks at a workbench (press E, then a number), and level up. There is a backpack and an inventory screen (I). Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running game.

    Shadows.

    Hills, trees, workbenches and the character now cast shadows from the sun, and faint ones from the moon. Checked by tests of the maths; not yet seen in the running window.

    Hills.

    The ground is no longer flat. In the editor, pick Sculpt terrain and drag to raise hills, dig pits, smooth bumps or flatten a spot to build on. Trees, grass and the player all stand on the hills, the mouse clicks land on the hillside you see, and the running game shows your changes as you sculpt. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    Sun, moon and sky.

    The world is now lit. There is a sun that rises in the east and sets in the west, warm light at dawn and dusk, a dark blue night with a moon and stars, and haze that fades far things into the sky. In the editor, open Map settings and drag the time slider to watch it. A map can also run a whole day in a chosen number of minutes. The colours are first guesses and I have not yet seen them in the running window.

    Changes show up without restarting.

    Press Play in the editor and keep working: whatever you place, move or paint appears in the running game within a moment. The game also reloads its map whenever the map file is saved, and the editor picks up files changed by other programs. A map with a mistake is refused and the old one keeps running. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running windows.

    Edit the game content from the editor.

    In the Content tab you can now switch on Edit mode and change items, tools, trees, workbenches and recipes with forms, add new ones, copy, rename (everything that used the old name is updated) and delete. The editor refuses to save a pack with a mistake in it and checks that what it writes reads back the same. While testing this I found and fixed two bugs in the content reader: negative numbers were being turned into zero, and a formula starting with a bracket would not read. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    A content browser in the editor.

    A new Content tab lists everything the game is made of: skills, items, tools, trees, workbenches, recipes and more. Search it, click an item to see its numbers and what uses it, and press Use in map to place it. Reload pack picks up edits to the content file without restarting. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    Handles and a built-in guide.

    Pick something in the editor and arrows, a ring or a square appear on it. Drag the arrows to move it, the ring to turn it, or the square to make it bigger or smaller, and press 1, 2 or 3 to switch. There is also a new ? Help button that opens a plain guide to everything the editor can do. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    Pick many things at once in the editor.

    Hold Ctrl or Shift and click to add things, or drag a box over empty ground to grab everything inside it. Then move, delete, copy, paste or duplicate them together. Closing the editor with unsaved changes now asks what to do. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    Undo and typing in the editor.

    The editor now takes keyboard typing, so you can name a map, set the ground size, save a copy under a new name or start a fresh map. Undo and redo work for placing, moving, deleting and changing things (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y), and Ctrl+S saves. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window.

    The editor can edit a map.

    The editor now opens on the starter map. You can fly around, pick a tree, workbench or rock from a list, click the ground to place it, click a thing to select it, drag it to move it, and delete it. Save writes the map to its file, and Play starts the game on your current map. Checked by tests; not yet tried in the running window. There is no undo and no typing yet.

    The game can load a map.

    A map is a short text file that lists where trees, workbenches and rocks go. The game now opens a starter map, sets the ground size and start point from it, and shows each thing as a simple coloured box. The checker tool can test a map too. Checked by tests; not yet looked at in the running game.

    The game and the editor become two programs.

    The game now opens with no menus and quits with Esc. The character creator moved into a new program called the editor, started with launch-editor.bat. This is the first step toward a place where the world gets built. Checked by tests and a clean build; not yet looked at in the running windows.

    Tools brought up to date.

    Rust moved to 1.99 and the small timing helper to its 1.0 release. Every other building block was already on its newest version. Physics will use one engine only, called rapier. All checks still pass.

    The site splits into pages.

    One long page became six short ones: Home, Status, Gallery, Try it, Log and Words.

    A Female look, smoother shoulders and arms.

    Choosing Female now adds a bust shape and offers four new hair styles: ponytail, bob, pigtails and braid. The shoulders were reshaped so the sleeves join the body, the shading is smoother, the arms hang closer to the body, and the hips now flow into the belt without a ledge. Checked in pictures and tests; not yet looked at in the running game.

    This site goes up.

    A plain summary of the project, kept up to date as the work moves.

    The Goose hat.

    First wearable item, built and checked over several rounds until it sat well on the head.

    Walking and Test mode.

    Walk with the keyboard, camera follows, and the Test button for mouse look.

    The foundation.

    The project structure, the .goose content language with a checker, gathering and crafting rules with tests, the window, and the first character creator.
